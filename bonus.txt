LogP helps determine the hydrophilic or lipophilic nature of a compound:

Higher LogP → more soluble in non-polar (organic) solvents.

Lower or negative LogP → more soluble in polar solvents (like water).

For example, CID 702 (Ethanol) has LogP ~ -0.1, meaning it's water-miscible.

CID 2244 (Aspirin) has LogP ~ 1.2, indicating some lipid solubility — useful in oral drug formulations.

Understanding these trends guides pharmaceutical chemists in choosing suitable solvents for drug solubilization and formulation.
